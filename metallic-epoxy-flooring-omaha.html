What Is Metallic Epoxy Flooring?

Metallic epoxy is a decorative coating where metallic pigments are suspended in clear resin and then moved across the floor with rollers, brushes, and air, creating depth and movement that reads like polished stone or poured marble. No two floors come out the same, because the pattern is formed by how the installer works the material while it's wet.

It's a finish, not a workhorse. Omaha customers put it in finished basements, retail showrooms, salons and studios, restaurant dining rooms, offices, and the occasional garage where the car is a hobby rather than transportation. The appeal is that it looks like nothing else and costs a fraction of real stone.

The trade-off worth knowing up front: metallic depends almost entirely on installer skill. The product doesn't produce the effect, the person applying it does. A crew doing their third metallic floor will hand you something that looks like a muddy puddle.

Finished Basements

The most common request we get. It makes a basement read as intentional living space rather than a converted utility area, and it handles the occasional water event far better than carpet.

Collector and hobby garages where the floor is part of the display. Needs a heavy-duty topcoat if a car actually parks on it.

Bathrooms and Wet Areas

Seamless and waterproof, with aggregate broadcast into the topcoat for grip. Popular in basement bathrooms and locker rooms.

How Is Metallic Different From Standard Epoxy?

Standard flake epoxy is a system: base coat, chips, topcoat, predictable result. Metallic is closer to a finishing trade. The pigment has to be worked while the resin is still open, and the window is short. Once it starts to set, whatever pattern is there is the pattern you keep.

That changes what matters when you're hiring. With flake, prep quality is nearly everything. With metallic, prep still matters just as much, and then installer experience matters on top of it. Ask to see photos of floors the actual crew has done, not manufacturer marketing shots.

It also changes durability expectations. The metallic layer sits under a clear topcoat, and that topcoat is what takes wear. On a floor with real traffic we build it with polyaspartic rather than standard polyurethane, which costs more and holds up considerably better against scratching and UV.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you match a specific color or design for my Omaha floor?

+

We can match a color family and give you a general direction, but we cannot reproduce a specific photo exactly. Metallic patterns form as the resin moves and sets, so every pour is unique. What we do is bring physical samples, agree on colors and how dramatic you want the movement, and then work within that. Anyone promising to duplicate a Pinterest photo precisely is overselling.

How durable is metallic epoxy compared to flake?

+

The metallic layer itself is protected by the clear topcoat, so durability comes down to which topcoat you choose. With a polyaspartic topcoat it holds up well to household and retail traffic for 10 to 15 years. In a working garage with road salt and heavy use, flake still outperforms it, mostly because flake hides wear and dirt that metallic shows.

Does metallic epoxy get slippery?

+

It's smooth by default, which means it's slick when wet. For bathrooms, entryways, basements near a sump, or any commercial space, we broadcast a fine aggregate into the topcoat to add grip. It changes the feel slightly underfoot and barely affects the look. For a dry finished basement, most Omaha customers skip it.

Can metallic epoxy go over an existing coating?

+

Only if the existing coating is fully bonded and we can mechanically abrade it to accept new material. In practice, most old coatings in Omaha basements and garages are already failing somewhere, and going over them just buries the problem. We test adhesion before deciding, and if it needs to come off, we grind it off.

How long does a metallic epoxy install take?

+

Typically two to three days on site: grinding and repair on day one, the metallic pour on day two, topcoat on day three. Cure times mean you're walking on it after about 24 hours from the final coat, with furniture back after 72. It takes longer than a flake floor because each layer has to fully set before the next.

Will the pattern look the same across the whole floor?

+

It will be consistent in color and character but varied in detail, which is the intended effect. Large open areas develop more dramatic movement than small rooms, and edges near walls behave differently from the center. If you want something completely uniform, a solid color or flake system is the better choice.
